Status

C05's progression model and first tuning pass are implemented. The reference
campaign estimates 19.04 hours for Big Piney, 18.65 for Desert and 23.07 for Beach:
60.76 hours total. These are expected-value estimates, not observed player times.
C08/G04 still require unfamiliar players and a full fresh-save completion run.

The model does not add mandatory wait timers, completion gates or course resets.
Players can unlock other courses early and choose their own order. The reference
route completes the three final targets in sequence, with carried-over progress.

Changes in the game

1. Ball Speed keeps its existing starting prices through level 60. Later prices
   use a bounded-growth campaign curve instead of continuing exponential growth.
   Beyond level 265 the price rises linearly. Shot strength, target locations,
   target health, XP rewards and the definition of completion are unchanged.
   This replaces financially prohibitive upgrades with an affordable route to
   the Mountain, Pyramids and Lighthouse.

2. Medal upgrade prices now fit the record-based retirement reward scale:
   Multi Ball: 100, 175, 250 medals (three upgrades).
   Consistency Mult: 25, 50, 75 ... 250 medals (ten upgrades).
   Multi Ball Spread: 20, 30, 40 ... 70 medals (six upgrades).
   Flat Distance: 8 medals initially, increasing by 4 per level.
   Existing caps/effects remain. Cash/medal purchases use the correct currency;
   direct purchase calls now enforce caps and demo restrictions as well as UI calls.

3. Market growth no longer compounds at 20% every second. It grows at 0.02% per
   second, with a shared $5/second ceiling across all pots. Growth transitions
   smoothly from compounding to a fixed maximum rate. Time-step-independent
   integration prevents FPS or splitting investments from multiplying interest.
   New deposit streak bonuses stop increasing at 10x. Existing saved pots and
   streak counts are retained; no accrued investment is removed or reset.
   No offline earnings are added. Save schema remains 3.

4. EconomyRules.gd is the shared price schedule used by buying, refunds and price
   displays. Retirement calculations are centralized there; their existing
   record-based formula and soft cap are retained. Repeating the same record
   still earns no medals. Help text reflects the new prices and Market limits.

Existing saves

Money, XP, levels, equipment, medals, investments and completion are preserved.
Owned upgrade levels are not reduced. Buying and refunding use the new schedule;
historical prices were never stored, so refunds use the current cost for the
refunded level. A buy/refund round trip creates no money, XP or retirement record.
An old save with a large investment keeps it; use the fresh playtest launcher to
evaluate the new campaign from zero rather than treating an old rich save as a
fresh-start pacing measurement.

Design milestones (A02)

First affordable purchase: within the first minute with regular interaction.
First retirement target: approximately 15-30 minutes; model chooses 20 minutes.
Early course/equipment access: approximately 30-60 minutes for Course 2 and early
golfers, around 2-3 hours for Course 3 and stronger retained equipment. Access does
not mean beating the course. Model's second retirement is at two hours, if its
record improvement earns at least 100 medals. Later retirements follow course
completion. These are planning targets, never forced retirement timers.
Final targets: roughly 20 hours of running-game progression per course, ~60 total.
Time closed does not count; background/suspend policy is still D07.

Model and reproducibility

python tests/run_economy_tests.py --godot <path-to-Godot-4.6.exe>

This command makes a clean full-game copy, isolates all profile directories,
imports it, and exports the live catalogs, upgrade prices, stat samples, target
hitboxes, respawn delays, flag data and physics settings. The Python model reads
that snapshot. Source hashes, JSON/CSV results, runtime logs and the physics
calibration are written under .test-output/economy/<timestamp>/.

For an additional planning experiment on an existing snapshot:
python tools/simulate_progression.py <economy-snapshot.json> --output <result.json>
Options include --policy balanced|speed|income, --cadence (purchase/aim checks in
seconds), --income-factor and --max-hours. --power/--scale are analysis-only price
experiments; they do not change the actual game's schedule.

The model uses linear-drag ballistics, legal aim/loft limits, whiffs, distance
variation and a 24-sample integration over the actual final-target hitboxes.
Target hits accumulate at their expected rate; rewards are credited only when
the target is destroyed, then the authored respawn delay is observed. Damage
and pending rewards are cleared on retirement, as in the scene reload.
Money/XP, level bonuses, current-run records, medal spending, equipment and cash
upgrade resets carry through the campaign. Purchases require earned currency.

The reference route buys upgrades using marginal utility per dollar, chooses
rewarding reachable targets, saves medals for course access and Lion Trees, and
uses Standard then Wrecking Ball. It buys at most three levels of each modeled
medal upgrade. Every minute it revisits purchases and aim; another scenario
checks every five minutes. Only center balls count toward target damage; fan
balls add estimated landing income. There are no free stats or partial-health
fixtures in the progression model. Physics calibration supplies the model's
earned state directly to isolate the accuracy of its shot prediction.

Approximation boundaries

This is a steady-state expected-value model, not a 60-hour replay of every shot.
It omits travel delays in income, uses continuous rather than discrete-frame drag,
approximates collisions/rebound landings, and credits expected fractional hits.
It assumes competent target selection and aim. It does not simulate discovery,
menu time, missed purchases, session interruptions or partial damage lost on quit.
It does not reproduce Homing steering, Firework fragment trajectories, Pinball
chains, golden-flag selection or a Market-focused strategy over the full campaign.
Their reward-routing regressions still run. Income sensitivity scenarios show
how additional bonuses or lower effective earnings move the pacing; they are
not claimed to be exact simulations of those special balls.

With the same refined purchase/retirement policy, the old schedule reaches Big

unfinished at the 120-hour horizon. Earlier exploratory policies stalled sooner.
ECONOMY_BASELINE.json records the comparable run and original snapshot hash.
That baseline excludes the old runaway Market strategy; it is not a claim that
no possible old-game strategy could finish faster.

Validation

207 economy checks cover live UI purchases/refunds, curve boundaries, cash/medal
currency, caps, retirement records, demo restrictions, existing investment values,
streak limits, growth ceilings, split-pot behavior and tick-size independence.
Nine real-physics checks exercise 32 swings per course using the simulated earned
stats and legal controls. Final-target hit rates were 32/32, 31/32 and 31/32 versus
the model's ~98%; no extra speed or partial target damage was supplied.
Model stat/reward/retirement samples are checked against the actual Godot runtime
to catch formula drift. Reference design bands are 16-24 hours per course and
55-65 hours overall. The source release gate now requires these economy checks.

Fresh balance testing

The Windows build includes a balance-playtest folder. Run Play Balance Test.cmd
to play the normal full game with a separate, initially empty profile. It supplies
no free currency, upgrades, automatic purchases or damaged targets. Reopen the
same launcher to continue. Reset Game clears only that profile.
The completion-test launcher is still available for quick milestone/save checks;
its boosted fixture must not be used to measure progression pacing.
